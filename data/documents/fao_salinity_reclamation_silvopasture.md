# FAO Soils Bulletin: Biosaline Agroforestry, Halophytic Forages, and Salinization Mitigation

**Source Reference**: FAO Soils Bulletin No. 74, *Soil Salinity Assessment: Methods and interpretation of electrical conductivity measurements*; Dagar, J. C. (2014), *Greening saline and waterlogged lands with agroforestry*, Agroforestry Systems, 88(6), 1013-1025.

## Key Scientific Findings and Multi-Metric Linkages
1. **Secondary Salinization Mechanism**: In irrigated arid/semi-arid rangelands, excessive tillage and shallow water tables cause capillary rise of mineral salts, driving electrical conductivity (ECe) > 8 dS/m and exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP) > 15%. This creates osmotic stress, collapses soil structural aggregates, and causes near-total loss of native plant guilds.
2. **Biosaline Silvopastoral Restoration**:
   - Integrating deep-rooted, drought- and salt-tolerant perennials (*Prosopis cineraria*, *Acacia nilotica*, *Casuarina equisetifolia*) along contour ridges acts as a biological pump, lowering the saline groundwater table by **0.8 to 1.5 meters** over 36 months.
   - Inter-row seeding of halophytic perennial shrubs (*Atriplex canescens*, *Atriplex nummularia*) and salt-tolerant legumes (*Melilotus indicus*) accumulates excess salts in foliar tissue (extracting up to 1.2 t salt/ha/yr) while yielding high-protein emergency fodder.
3. **Biological Recovery Metrics**: Surface ECe drops by **35% to 55%** in the top 30 cm layer, soil microbial dehydrogenase activity doubles, and native grassland bird nesting density increases by **+80%** due to structural cover.
4. **Time Horizon**: Water table drawdown requires **2 to 3 years**; soil desalting and microbial stabilization requires **4 to 6 years (long-term)**.
