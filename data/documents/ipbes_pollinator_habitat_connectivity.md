# IPBES Global Assessment: Floral Diversity, Wild Pollinators, and Trophic Cascades in Agricultural Landscapes

**Source Reference**: Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES), *Thematic Assessment Report on Pollinators, Pollination and Food Production*, Bonn, Germany (2016); Garibaldi, L. A. et al. (2016), *Mutually beneficial pollinator diversity and crop yield in small and large farms*, Science, 351(6271), 388-391.

## Key Scientific Findings and Multi-Metric Linkages
1. **Pollination Deficit in Monocultures**: Continuous monocultures (e.g., wheat, maize) lack floral rewards (nectar, pollen) and nesting substrates for solitary wild bees (*Andrena*, *Halictus*, *Osmia*) and hoverflies (*Syrphidae*). The pollinator visitation rate in unbroken cereal monocultures typically drops below **5 visits per 100 m² per hour**, inducing local extinctions of pollinator guilds.
2. **Wildflower Strips and Flowering Hedgerow Thresholds**: Devoting **8% to 12%** of cropland area to perennial, native flowering buffer strips or agroforestry hedgerows:
   - Boosts wild bee abundance by **+200% to +350%** across adjacent field centers within 500 meters.
   - Restores continuous phenological food supply from early spring through autumn.
   - Reduces crop pest damage by **16% to 30%** via conservation biological control by sheltering predatory ground beetles (*Carabidae*) and parasitoid wasps (*Ichneumonoidea*).
3. **Soil-Pollinator Interdependence**: Over 70% of wild native bee species nest directly in the ground. Heavy tillage and compact, carbon-depleted soils crush underground brood chambers. Transitioning to zero-till intercropping or agroforestry directly restores soil nesting cavities, increasing solitary bee emergence rates by **+65%**.
4. **Time Horizon**: Pollinator foraging activity increases within **1 growing season**; solitary bee nesting densities reach steady-state recovery within **3 to 4 years**.
