# USDA NRCS: Soil Organic Matter, Available Water Capacity (AWC), and Hydrological Resilience

**Source Reference**: United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS), *Soil Health Technical Note No. 19: Soil Organic Matter and Available Water Capacity*, Washington D.C.; Hudson, B. D. (1994), *Soil organic matter and available water capacity*, Journal of Soil and Water Conservation, 49(2), 189-194.

## Key Scientific Findings and Multi-Metric Equations
1. **The Organic Matter - Water Retention Multiplier**: Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) is the primary governing agent of micro- and meso-porosity in topsoil. For mineral soils, every 1% increase in SOC (equivalent to ~1.72% soil organic matter) increases the Available Water Capacity (AWC) by **18,000 to 27,000 gallons per acre** (approximately 170,000 to 250,000 liters of water per hectare) in the top 30 cm profile.
2. **Infiltration Rate vs. Runoff**: Depleted soils with SOC < 0.5% develop surface crusts under rain events, causing water infiltration rates to drop below 5 mm/hour, producing up to 60% surface runoff and severe rill erosion. Restoring SOC to 1.5% via mulch/cover cropping elevates infiltration rates to **25-45 mm/hour**, converting surface runoff into stored soil moisture.
3. **Soil Faunal Catalysts**: Elevated SOC provides the primary food substrate for anecic earthworms and burrowing arthropods. Macropores created by earthworm biopores increase unsaturated hydraulic conductivity by **3-fold to 5-fold**, extending plant-available moisture during dry spells by **10 to 18 additional days**.
4. **Time Horizon**: Infiltration improvements are observed within 12 months; full hydrological holding capacity gains require 3-5 years of continuous non-disturbance.
